# 2026 ASA DataQuest: UN World Population Prospects 2024 EDA

Comprehensive exploratory data analysis, data cleaning pipeline, and research questions for the United Nations World Population Prospects (WPP 2024) dataset across 237 countries and areas (1950–2100).

## 🎯 Submission: "Already Baked In"

**Question.** How do momentum (today's age structure), mortality, fertility and migration combine to move each country's
population from 2024 to 2100 — and is any single indicator enough?

**Method.** Four of the UN's own projection variants form a chain (Andreev, Kantorová & Bongaarts 2013):
`Momentum → Instant-replacement-zero-migration → Zero migration → Medium`. Each step is a ratio, so
`P2100/P2024 = momentum × mortality × fertility × migration` holds exactly for all 237 countries (no residual).

**Headline results** (all numbers regenerate from `analysis/tables/decomp_numbers.json`):
- World 8.13 bn → 10.17 bn = ×1.18 momentum × 1.11 mortality × 0.95 fertility × 1.01 migration. Fertility is already a brake.
- Replacement fertility from 2024 would still end at 10.68 bn — above the UN medium path.
- 131 countries are below replacement in 2024; 51 of them (2.6 bn people) still have growth-pushing age structures; 26 still grow by 2100.
- Fertility explains 59% of cross-country variance in growth, momentum 32%, migration 9% (1% when population-weighted).
- Robustness: a second chain of UN runs gives r = 0.99 for fertility and migration effects; the main driver is unchanged for 97% of countries.
- Uncertainty: the UN 95% interval for 2100 spans ×5.9 for the median country; its width tracks population size (ρ = −0.67), not the growth engine.

## 🚀 Key Highlights & Findings

1. **Double-Counting Disentangled:**
   - Raw WPP file mixes 237 sovereign nations with 318 aggregate groupings (regional, income, ad-hoc alliances). Naively summing all rows inflated global population to **44.8×** reality.
   - Cleaned panel strictly isolates the **237 countries**, reconciling with UN global totals with $< 3.63 \times 10^{-7}$ error.
2. **Global Turning Points:**
   - Global population peaks at **10.29 billion in 2084**, up from 2.47B (1950) and 8.06B (2023).
   - Africa's share of world population expands from **9.1% (1950) $\rightarrow$ 37.4% (2100)**; Europe contracts from **22.1% $\rightarrow$ 5.8%**.
3. **Sub-Replacement Timing:**
   - In 2023, 130 countries (67.3% of world population) are below replacement ($TFR < 2.1$). By 2100, 224 countries (87.0% of world population) are sub-replacement.
4. **Statistical Break (2023 / 2024):**
   - Estimates (1950–2023) contain stochastic empirical noise ($\text{median } |\Delta LEx| = 0.29$ yr/yr), whereas UN projections (2024–2100) are deterministic and smoothed ($\text{median } |\Delta LEx| = 0.16$ yr/yr, $p < 10^{-120}$).
